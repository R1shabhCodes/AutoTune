Income Tax in India: A Complete Guide

India follows a progressive tax system where individuals are taxed based on their income slabs. As of the financial year 2024-25, there are two tax regimes available to taxpayers: the Old Regime and the New Regime.

New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023-24):
The new tax regime offers lower tax rates but with fewer deductions. The tax slabs under the new regime are:
- Income up to Rs 3 lakh: No tax (nil rate)
- Income from Rs 3 lakh to Rs 7 lakh: 5% tax rate
- Income from Rs 7 lakh to Rs 10 lakh: 10% tax rate
- Income from Rs 10 lakh to Rs 12 lakh: 15% tax rate
- Income from Rs 12 lakh to Rs 15 lakh: 20% tax rate
- Income above Rs 15 lakh: 30% tax rate

A standard deduction of Rs 75,000 is available under the new regime for salaried individuals. Additionally, a rebate under Section 87A is available for income up to Rs 7 lakh, making the effective tax zero for incomes up to this threshold.

Old Tax Regime:
The old tax regime allows various deductions and exemptions:
- Income up to Rs 2.5 lakh: No tax
- Income from Rs 2.5 lakh to Rs 5 lakh: 5%
- Income from Rs 5 lakh to Rs 10 lakh: 20%
- Income above Rs 10 lakh: 30%

Key Deductions under the Old Regime:
Section 80C allows deductions up to Rs 1.5 lakh for investments in PPF, ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premiums, EPF contributions, NSC, tax-saving fixed deposits, and tuition fees for children.

Section 80D allows deductions for health insurance premiums. The limit is Rs 25,000 for individuals below 60 years and Rs 50,000 for senior citizens. An additional Rs 25,000 to Rs 50,000 can be claimed for parents' health insurance.

HRA Exemption is available for salaried individuals living in rented accommodation. The exemption is calculated as the minimum of: actual HRA received, 50% of salary (metro cities) or 40% of salary (non-metro), or rent paid minus 10% of salary.

Section 80CCD(1B) allows an additional deduction of Rs 50,000 for contributions to the National Pension System (NPS), over and above the Rs 1.5 lakh limit of Section 80C.

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS):
Employers are required to deduct TDS from salaries based on the applicable tax slab. TDS on fixed deposits is deducted at 10% if interest exceeds Rs 40,000 per year (Rs 50,000 for senior citizens). Form 16 is a TDS certificate issued by the employer showing the tax deducted during the financial year.

Advance Tax:
Taxpayers with a tax liability exceeding Rs 10,000 in a financial year must pay advance tax in quarterly installments: 15% by June 15, 45% by September 15, 75% by December 15, and 100% by March 15.
